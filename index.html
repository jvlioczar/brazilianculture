Fancy a cafézinho?

This land belonged to more than a thousand peoples long before it was called Brazil. Today, 391 Indigenous peoples speak 295 languages here, alongside the descendants of Africans, Europeans, Arabs and Asians. And all of them will offer you something.

In Brazil, no visitor gets past the door without hearing this question: a little coffee, strong and sweet. Saying no is a bit rude, so let’s say you accepted. It’s the first Brazilian custom in this guide.

first, the land

A country the size of a continent

This is Brazil.

8.5 million km², the world’s 5th largest country and almost half of South America. It borders every country on the continent except Chile and Ecuador.

Now, with all of Europe.

From the Atlantic to the Urals, the continent covers about 10 million km², with more than 40 countries. Brazil alone has 8.5 million and is twice the size of the European Union.

213 million against 745 million.

A single country with the world’s 7th largest population and one main language, on a territory almost the size of a whole continent. It’s the largest Portuguese-speaking country on the planet.

Clinging to the sea, like crabs.

The image comes from a 17th-century friar and still holds: 54.8% of people live within 150 km of the coast. Each dot is one of the 25 biggest cities. Notice how empty the Amazon is.

Sources: IBGE, population estimate (2025), IBGE, 2022 Census, UN, population of Europe and Natural Earth (map).

    Who are the Brazilians?

    Out of every 100 people, 55 identify as Black or mixed-race.

    Indigenous peoples, almost 5 million Africans brought by force and immigrants from Portugal, Italy, Germany, Lebanon and Japan created one of the most mixed populations in the world. It has the largest population of African descent outside Africa and the largest Japanese community outside Japan.
